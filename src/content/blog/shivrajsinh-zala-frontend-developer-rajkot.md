I build websites in Rajkot, Gujarat. Mostly custom WordPress themes and Squarespace 7.1 sites, some React and Astro frontends, and a fair amount of scroll-driven animation work with GSAP. Around 30 client sites so far, built and maintained.

This page exists because people who hear my name from a referral usually want to know three things before they call: what I actually do, whether I have done it before, and how working with me goes. So here it is, without the marketing gloss.

## What I do

My work splits into four categories, roughly in the order I spend time on them.

**Custom WordPress theme development.** Themes written from scratch — no Elementor, no Divi, no 40-plugin stack held together with hope. This includes WooCommerce builds with Indian payment gateways, and a steady stream of rescue jobs on sites someone else built in a page builder that now take eight seconds to load on a phone.

**Squarespace 7.1 design and development.** Custom design work inside Squarespace: real CSS, real code injection, layouts the native editor cannot produce on its own. The goal is always a site that does not look like a Squarespace template, while your team still edits everything in the normal Squarespace editor.

**Frontend engineering.** React, Astro and Tailwind CSS for projects that need an application rather than a website. This site itself is built in Astro.

**Motion and interaction.** GSAP and ScrollTrigger for scroll-driven interfaces. The discipline that matters here is restraint — animation that costs you your Interaction to Next Paint score is a net loss no matter how good it looks in a demo video.

## Background

I did my BCA at OMVVIM College in Morbi under Saurashtra University, then my MCA at B H Gardi College of Engineering & Technology in Rajkot under GTU. Two years in Morbi is why I understand the ceramic and tile trade well enough to build for it — the buyer behaviour there is genuinely different from anything else in Gujarat.

Since February 2025 I have been a Frontend Developer at Just Digital Gurus in Rajkot, where I develop and maintain the client website portfolio, architect custom WordPress themes, and implement Figma and Sketch designs in Squarespace and Webflow. Alongside that I take on freelance projects directly.

Not the prettiest projects — the ones that solved something.

**Venus Pumps** and **Solar Quartz** are Rajkot manufacturer sites where the real problem was catalogue structure, not visual design. Getting specification tables, downloadable datasheets and product hierarchy right is what makes an export buyer send an enquiry instead of closing the tab.

## How I work

**Fixed scope, fixed price, fixed date.** We talk once about what the site needs to do commercially — enquiries, orders, credibility ahead of a buyer visit. Then you get a written scope with a price and a delivery date. I do not bill hourly, so overruns are my problem rather than yours.

**You watch it get built.** Everything happens on a staging URL you can open any time. Review happens at wireframe, at design and at build. No reveal at the end, no surprises.

**You own everything at handover.** Hosting in your account, domain in your name, admin access that is genuinely yours, and a walkthrough document. If you hire someone else next year, they inherit a working site with commented code and no mysteries. I have picked up too many projects where the previous developer held the hosting hostage to ever do that to a client.

**I tell you when you do not need me.** If your problem is a ₹15,000 fix rather than a rebuild, I will say so. If Squarespace is the right answer and I would earn more building you a WordPress site, I will still say Squarespace. That costs me projects occasionally and it is still the correct trade.

## What I turn down

Being specific about this is more useful than claiming to do everything.

I do not take on native mobile app development, backend-heavy platform work, or anything needing a dedicated DevOps practice. I do not do link-buying, PBNs or any SEO tactic that depends on Google not noticing — sites I build should still be ranking in three years.

I also turn down projects where the brief is "make it look like this competitor" with no thought about what the site is commercially for. Those never end well for either side.
